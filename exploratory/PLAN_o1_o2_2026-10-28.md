### 1.2 Two calendars, neither primary

Both are run in full and reported side by side in one table; the text never ranks them or chooses the more favourable.

**Daily.** Reference 7 Aug (day 59). Pre: days 0–58 (9 Jun–6 Aug), 59 coefficients. Post: 9 Aug–12 Sep, 35 coefficients; 8 Aug is absent (part-treated, excluded by the prespec). HonestDiD treats periods as equally spaced, so the 7→9 Aug step is one index step spanning two calendar days. Disclosed consequence: under smoothness, a trend linear in calendar days has second difference equal to its slope at that junction, so index-linear extrapolation understates a calendar-linear trend by one day's slope per post day (≈ 0.002 at the pre slope). Target: the equal-weight mean of the 35 post coefficients (D −0.1031 against 7 Aug).

**Weekly.** Bins as in `o3_weekly_bins.csv`: pre 9–19 Jun (11 days, stub merged), then six Monday–Sunday weeks 20 Jun–31 Jul, reference 1–7 Aug (7 days); post 9–14 Aug (6 days, 8 Aug missing), three full weeks 15 Aug–4 Sep, 5–12 Sep (8 days, stub merged). Bin coefficient = day-count mean of the daily coefficients in the bin (C β), covariance C Σ Cᵀ (O3 verified C β against a weekly-binned refit: max |diff| < 1e-10; the off-diagonal blocks of C Σ Cᵀ are correct by construction and were not separately reviewed). Recentring: every bin coefficient minus the reference-bin coefficient (matrix applied to both β and Σ), after which the reference row is dropped. 7 pre bins, 5 post bins. Target: day-count-weighted post mean, weights (6, 7, 7, 7, 8)/35, which equals the 35-day mean by identity; relative to the 1–7 Aug week it is D −0.1027 (vs −0.1031 daily, a 0.0003 gap from the different reference, disclosed). Equal bin weights are forbidden (on these five bins they move the D point estimate to −0.1035; the −0.0999 quoted earlier came from a superseded six-bin layout with a 1-day 12 Sep stub, refuter R8). The irregular end bins (11 and 8 days, and the 6-day first post bin that skips 8 Aug) are disclosed; M̄ is in bin-index units and HonestDiD treats the bins as equally spaced, which is an assumption to disclose, not one the method checks. Seat 1's dissent (keep the 4-day and 1-day stubs) is recorded in the report.

### 1.4 Thresholds and clearance

- Thresholds: 0 and −0.10 for D and for A (SPEC), plus −0.05 for A (δₐ; orchestrator decision: seat 1's round-2 proposal, a superset that costs only compute). Disclosure: −0.05 was added after O3's A intervals existed; its M̄ = 0 clearance was therefore likely but not known (§0: the O3 intervals clear by ≈1.7 SE, but the RM C-LF interval at M̄ = 0 can be wider than the O3 interval).
- "Clears τ" means the robust interval's upper bound is strictly below τ. The −0.10 threshold is known to be degenerate for D and A (§0) and is still run and reported. The previews of §0 (RM sign breakdowns, SD M = 0 intervals, the O3 A intervals that make A M̄ = 0 clearance likely) are printed beside the O2 tables so that no previewed fact is read as new.
- **Both clusterings (orchestrator decision, carrying prespec §2's rule to O2; refuter R11):** every cell is computed and reported under wallet and under stratum clustering. A statement that a threshold "is cleared" at a given M̄ or M is made only if both clusterings clear it there. The joint breakdown of a cell is the smaller of the two clusterings' breakdowns; if either clustering has no initial clearance, the joint result is "no initial clearance". Disagreement between clusterings is never resolved in favour of either; the joint statement takes the weaker one and both are printed.
- D and A are separate cells with their own thresholds. A sentence about both outcomes together ("the gap and combined holdings") uses the weaker of the two joint breakdowns.

### 1.5 Breakdown rule

For each calendar × restriction × outcome × clustering × threshold:

1. Evaluate and report every grid point (interval, clears yes/no, numerical status).
2. If the first grid point (0) does not clear, report "no initial clearance" and stop for that cell.
3. Breakdown = the first grid point, in ascending order, that loses clearance. Bisect ten times inside the bracket [last clearing point, first losing point] only, and report the bracket after ten halvings. All bisection evaluations are reported.
4. Any later grid point that clears again is reported as non-monotone; no search continues past the first loss for a preferred value.
5. Runtime valve, fixed now (refuter R13 plan half; no addendum is needed to use it): timing call per calendar (one RM call at M̄ = 0 on the real cell, planned method and `gridPoints`; its interval is discarded unread). Projected cell time T = measured seconds × 50 (40 grid points + 10 bisections) × 1.5. **Trigger:** T > 2 h for any cell, or the sum over all cells > 8 h wall-clock after parallelism. **Fallbacks, in this order, stopping as soon as the rule passes:** (i) parallelise (one process per cell and/or `parallel = TRUE` over M̄; seed fixed, output identical — not a deviation of results); (ii) method `Conditional` instead of C-LF for the affected calendar (same size, C-LF only adds hybrid power; FLCI and C-F are not available for RM); the label is printed on every RM column; (iii) `gridPoints` lowered to no less than 200 with `grid.lb/ub` tightened to ±8 SD of l′β_post so the θ step stays ≈ 0.08 SD; the step is printed beside every interval and any "open endpoint" warning invalidates that grid; (iv) if daily RM still trips, daily RM is not computed and is reported as such; weekly RM stays. Shrinking the pre-window is forbidden (it changes the assumption). Source basis: HonestDiD source read and timed 28 Oct (S31h; weekly Conditional ≈ 20 s per M̄ point, daily Conditional @200 ≈ 124 s, daily C-LF @200 ≈ 427 s, cost linear in `gridPoints`). The timing call is made before any interval of that run is printed or read; the fallbacks are applied in the order named, one at a time, each to the whole affected calendar × restriction (never to single grid points). The fallback in force is recorded in the outputs with the measured time. Evaluations already made are reported regardless. Disclosed consequence: `gridPoints` sets the θ-grid step, i.e. the resolution of each interval's endpoints, not the M̄ spacing; accepted sets are reported by their outermost accepted grid points, so endpoints are biased inward by up to one step and a breakdown comes out slightly too small; the θ step is printed beside every interval. Departures from S31h's recommended list (disclosed): its "coarser M̄ grid" step is dropped because the M̄ grid is fixed by §1.5 step 1, and its last resort (shrink the pre-window) is replaced by "drop daily RM" because shrinking changes the assumption. S31h read and timed the 0.2.8 source; in 0.2.7 `deltarm.R` and `sensitivityresults.R` are byte-identical and `arp-nuisance.R`/`deltasd.R` differ only in the CVXR solve call (checked 28 Oct). Non-method arguments, pinned: `alpha = 0.05`, `seed = 0` (package default), RM `gridPoints = 1000` and `grid.lb/ub` = package default ±20 SD of l′β_post unless the valve applies, `postPeriodMomentsOnly = TRUE`, `parallel` per the valve; SD uses the package defaults of `createSensitivityResults` (method FLCI, which exposes no grid arguments).
6. If every grid point clears, report "no crossing within the grid" with the largest grid value. Numerical failures (solver error, empty or infinite interval) are reported as such and never treated as clear or lost; if a failure sits inside the bracket, the bisection reports where it failed.

### 1.6 Outputs

`out/exploratory/o2-<date>/`: one long csv (calendar, restriction, outcome, cluster, grid value, lo, hi, clears per threshold, status), one breakdown csv (one row per cell in §1.5, per clustering and joint), the original (unrestricted) intervals, the §0 previews beside them, input hashes (O3 files, signed outputs), runtime per grid point and the fallback in force, `sessionInfo` of the isolated library, `MANIFEST.sha256`, `summary.md`. ### 2.2 Extraction (new unbound code, `exploratory/o1_extract.py`)

- Pin: the last block with timestamp before 2022-03-01 00:00:00 UTC. Opening balance = USDC and USDT `balanceOf` at the pin (archive read). Then a Transfer walk (and USDT DestroyedBlackFunds) from pin + 1 through the last block of 9 Jun 2022 UTC, giving end-of-day balances in base units with the same day convention as `gates/g5_post.py` (end-of-UTC-day state). The O1 path uses the walked days 1 Mar–8 Jun; 9 Jun onward comes from the signed panel.
- **Anchor of the identity (refuter R9).** No signed 8-Jun balance exists: the signed hashes cover `wallet_coin_day_pre.csv`, whose `day_index 0` is the end-of-9-Jun state; `out/g4/balances_checkpoint.jsonl` is hashed nowhere and is not used as an anchor. The identity is therefore, per wallet and coin, tolerance 0: balanceOf(pin) + net walked flows over pin + 1 … last block of 9 Jun (this includes the 9-Jun flows, blocks 14,929,561 onward) = the signed `day_index 0` balance in `wallet_coin_day_pre.csv`.
- **Complete walk (refuter R12).** A wallet's walk is complete when both pin balances were read and every Transfer / DestroyedBlackFunds log range from pin + 1 to the last block of 9 Jun was fetched without a GapError or failed read, on the primary endpoint or, after one retry, on the second endpoint. The walk uses g4's unit of `WALK_ARRAY` = 1,000 addresses (imported from `gates/g4_controls.py`, never edited); a range failure inside a unit makes every wallet of that unit incomplete for that range until the retry succeeds. An incomplete wallet has a missing O1 path; counts by arm and weight share are reported.
- **Mismatch rule (refuter R12, orchestrator decision).** A complete wallet that fails the identity, whose reconstructed balance goes negative on any day, or that has no signed day-index-0 row is marked `mismatch`, excluded from every O1 fit and counted by arm. If mismatches exceed 0.5% of complete wallets in either arm, the run stops before any estimation: the cause is written into the addenda file (§3) and the extraction is re-run in full after a code fix; no estimate is computed from a run that stopped. At or below 0.5%, estimation proceeds with the exclusions and the per-arm counts and weight shares beside every table. Mismatch diagnostics are extraction facts, not estimates, and may be read before estimation.
- **Re-verify (refuter R10).** A seeded 5% sample of wallets (seed 808, drawn within arm) is re-read on a second endpoint: both pin `balanceOf` values and the full log ranges pin + 1 … last block of 9 Jun, compared by per-range log count and sha256 of the canonical log list (as `gates/g4_controls.py` `_reverify` does), plus the derived daily path; zero tolerance. A wallet that differs gets a missing O1 path. If more than 1% of the re-verified wallets differ in either arm, the run stops before estimation as in the mismatch rule.
- Receipts for every RPC call. A failed archive read is missing, never zero. A missing pin balance for either coin makes the wallet's O1 path missing for all of 1 Mar–8 Jun; counts by arm are reported.
- Outputs go only to `out/exploratory/o1-<date>/` (its own directory; nothing is written under `out/g4/`, `out/recovery/` or any signed output directory). Signed-bound files are read, never edited (SPEC post-results wave list). One acquisition slot.

### 2.4 Estimands and fixed bands

All are descriptive, from the capped coefficient path c_t (7 Aug = 0), for D and A, both clusterings where an interval exists.

- **E1 — jump rank.** The 7→9 Aug two-day move (D −0.0906, A −0.0809, from the signed path) ranked by absolute value among all two-calendar-day moves c_{t+2} − c_t with both days in 1 Mar–7 Aug; also its signed rank among negative moves, and its ratio to the SD of those moves and to the largest. No one-day comparisons. A rank is not a p-value.
- **E2 — dynamics.** SD of daily changes and their lag-1 autocorrelation; the AR(1) coefficient of the level path (OLS, c_t on c_{t−1} with intercept) over 1 Mar–7 Aug; and the random-walk band for the post mean against 7 Aug recomputed with the extended-window step SD by the O3 `rw_post_mean_band95_calendar` formula. E2 has no band; it is reported.
- **E3 — March level.** Level L = mean of c_t over 1 Mar–8 May (pre-Terra); slope = OLS slope over the same days (reported as a point estimate only, no band and no interval: orchestrator decision, interval type "none"). Bands on the point estimate of L: **high** if L ≥ +0.20; **low** if L ≤ +0.10; **between** otherwise. Orchestrator decision (refuter R1, SEV1): the bands are kept and renamed from "drift / no drift / inconclusive" to "high / low / between", because L cannot discriminate drift from a driftless random walk (§0: a driftless random walk lands in "high" 29% (D) / 37% (A) of the time; a continued drift 65–78% (D) / 75–85% (A)). These rates are printed beside every E3 result. The sampling interval of L (from the saved covariance) is reported beside it and labelled as ignoring group-level shocks; it does not enter the band. If L < 0 that is flagged descriptively (the gap below the window; a selection reading, seat 2). Disclosure: E3's last day (8 May) is the base day of the E4 secondary measure.
- **Stable.** Defined inside "low" by construction: stable ⇔ (E3 = low) and |L − m| ≤ 0.05, where m is the 9 Jun–7 Aug coefficient mean (D +0.0489, A +0.0403). The AR coefficient is not part of the definition (orchestrator decision: E2 is reported, not banded).
- **E4 — Terra.** Primary: the single Terra two-day move c(13 May) − c(11 May) (chg-c5371ff2 transcript: "the Terra 11–13 May move"). Secondary, labelled as such in every table: the largest two-calendar-day move (by absolute value, sign reported) with both days in 8–14 May, and the cumulative change c(13 May) − c(8 May). Orchestrator decision (refuter R7): the first version of this plan made the 8–14 May maximum the trigger without marking the change; the trigger is restored to the single move because a maximum over five windows mechanically favours the trigger. Trigger: if |primary Terra move| ≥ |7→9 Aug move| for the same outcome, the text may no longer call the 9-Aug move unusual for this gap. The secondary measures never fire the trigger; they are reported beside it.

### 2.5 Mandatory secondary row (age-restricted)

- Predicate, from state at the pin only: first_send_block ≤ pin (from the signed `covariates.csv`) OR USDC balanceOf(pin) > 0 OR USDT balanceOf(pin) > 0. It is evidence of activity or holdings by the pin, not a wallet-existence test. A wallet is admitted if any clause is true on the values that were read. A wallet for which no clause is true and at least one pin balance is missing (refuter R12: one or both) is "unknown" and excluded from the row; a wallet with all three clauses readable and false is not admitted. Counts reported for each case.
- Admissions reported by clause × arm.
- CEM weights recomputed by the prespec formula within the restricted set (same strata and cut-points); exposed wallets left without a control are dropped. The weight share dropped is reported by arm.
- **Estimands for this row (refuter R6).** The O1 fit ends at 7 Aug, so the row has no own 7→9 Aug move. E1 ranks the signed full-sample 7→9 Aug move against the restricted row's own two-day pre moves. E2, E3 (L, slope), E4 (primary and secondary) and m are computed from the restricted row's own coefficient path; "stable" for this row uses the row's own m.
- **Tie-break (downgrade only).** E3 is read from the full sample. If the restricted row's L falls in a different E3 band, the E3 reading is "between" (shown as "between: full and restricted rows disagree"), and §2.7 uses the "between" row. Stable is declared only if both the full sample and the restricted row are stable; otherwise, when E3 is "low" in both, the reading is "low, not stable". The E4 trigger fires if it fires in either the full sample or the restricted row. E1 and E2 for the row are reported beside the full-sample values with no tie-break.
- If the restricted row is not estimable (no exposed–control pair left, or the fit fails), it is reported as "restricted row not estimable" with the dropped weight share by arm, and E3 stays full-sample with that disclosure. It is never skipped silently.

### 2.6 Coverage, caps and clipping

Beside every O1 path, by month (Mar, Apr, May, 1–8 Jun) × arm, CEM-weighted: share of wallet-days with a valid balance, share holding zero combined, and clip incidence beyond each cap by tail (the signed `post_cap_clip.csv` definition extended to months). The uncapped D and A paths are shown with the capped ones. A capped March reading is not interpreted until these are on the same page. Orchestrator decision (refuter R12): coverage and clip incidence are reported without a threshold; no O1 reading is suppressed, changed or re-banded because of them, and the §2.7 reading is printed next to them so a reader can judge.

### 2.7 What each reading may and may not support

Written before any March balance is read. Every statement is exploratory and post-results; the signed H1 case (case 1 under both clusterings, pooled capped β) is unchanged by any of them.

Joint rule (orchestrator decision, refuter R11): each row is entered separately for D and for A, and the table is printed for both. A reading that the text attributes to the stablecoin position as a whole (the gap and combined holdings together) requires D and A to land in the same row; if they differ, that combined reading is "between" (the report's "inconclusive"; E3 "between" carries no reading beyond the paths and E1/E2/E4) and each outcome's row is reported alone under its own name. E3 bands use point estimates, so clustering does not enter them; any interval quoted in a reading must hold under both clusterings, or the weaker one is quoted.

| O1 pattern (full sample, after the tie-break) | Reading it supports | What it cannot support |
