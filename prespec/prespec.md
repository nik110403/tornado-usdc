### 1a. USDC share in matching (Amendment A2)

USDC share at baseline is almost the baseline level of the outcome, and matching on it can cause regression toward the mean when the groups differ at baseline (Daw & Hatfield 2018). Leaving it out lets the groups differ in coin mix, which exposes β to any market-wide USDC shock. With 71,784 control-frame wallets, A1's two tests would almost surely have dropped it. The fixed rule is therefore:

- USDC share = the ratio of baseline sums, USDC ÷ (USDC + USDT), over the 60 baseline days.
- It enters CEM in three bins, classified before rounding: exactly 0, strictly between 0 and 1, exactly 1. These bins are the same under a mean-of-daily-ratios definition.
- A1's diagnostics (a) and (b) are computed and reported, and select nothing. Their results *(pipeline records)*: (a) mean USDC share 0.784 among 4,501 eligible exposed wallets against 0.352 among 71,779 frame wallets; only USDC 58.6% against 24.6%, no USDC 13.8% against 55.9%. (b) On 63,322 frame wallets with both half-period shares defined (8,457 excluded), the slope of the second-half-minus-first-half change on the first-half share is −0.0699 and the correlation −0.181. Both are descriptive.
- Robustness rows (§5 item 6): matching without USDC share; and USDC share coarsened at the eligible exposed wallets' quartiles. Each is rematched under the same rules and reports its retained exposed population.

## 2. Outcomes and estimating equations

- H1 outcome: $D_{it} = (\text{USDC}_{it} - \text{USDT}_{it}) / B_i$, with $B_i$ = wallet $i$'s mean combined USDC + USDT holdings over the baseline. Tokens valued at par.
- H1a outcome: $A_{it} = (\text{USDC}_{it} + \text{USDT}_{it}) / B_i$.
- Capping. D and A are each capped at the 1st and 99th percentiles of their own values over all baseline wallet-days of the final matched sample, both groups pooled, unweighted, R quantile type 7. The same values cap every day, before and after the event. Capped D decides H1 and capped A decides H1a; H1 therefore concerns the capped normalised gap. Cut-points *(pipeline records)*: D [-3.0927, 4.4768], A [0, 5.3170], over 958,920 baseline wallet-days.
- Uncapped veto. H1 is supported only if capped D is in case 1 (§3) **and** neither the wallet- nor the stratum-clustered uncapped D interval is in case 3, 4 or 5 (that is, neither lies wholly above −δ). If capped D is in case 1 and the veto fires, H1 is reported as cap-sensitive, not supported.
- Clip incidence. For each cap, the share of wallet-days strictly beyond it is reported by arm, tail and period, weighted. H1 is flagged cap-sensitive if, at either tail of either cap, the exposed-minus-control difference in the post-minus-baseline change of that share exceeds 1 percentage point. A cap-sensitive flag is reported beside the H1 decision and blocks the §3 reallocation wording; it does not change the H1 case.
- Equation, for D and A: $Y_{it} = \beta(\text{Exposed}_i \times \text{Post}_t) + \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \varepsilon_{it}$, wallet and day fixed effects, CEM weights. Parallel trends is assumed for the capped outcomes, which is not the same assumption as for the uncapped ones.
- Inference. Conditional on the selected matched sample and its fixed CEM weights, the intervals allow arbitrary serial dependence within a wallet and assume independence across wallets; they are not robust to common ownership, shared group-specific shocks or the uncertainty from matching. Wallet-clustered SEs ignore the estimation of the CEM weights and ran about 4% too small on synthetic data; this is reported, not corrected. Intervals are computed clustered by wallet and, separately, by CEM stratum. **H1 is supported only if both the wallet-clustered and the stratum-clustered intervals are in case 1** (and the veto above does not fire); if they disagree, the decision is reported as dependence-sensitive, and neither is chosen for being favourable. Cluster counts, sizes and weight concentration are reported. Owner-grouped inference is not estimable: no owner partition can be built defensibly from the frozen resources (the Tornado Cash linking heuristics are noisy and unvalidated on USDC pools; exchange labels do not exist). No address is linked merely because it used the same pool.
- Legs, descriptive: the same equation with uncapped $\text{USDC}_{it}/B_i$ and $\text{USDT}_{it}/B_i$, on the primary sample, dates and weights. On uncapped outcomes, $\beta_{USDC} = (\beta_A + \beta_D)/2$ and $\beta_{USDT} = (\beta_A - \beta_D)/2$ hold exactly; they do not hold for the capped D and A. A switch through a centralised exchange shows as a USDC exit with no USDT gain on-chain, so the legs do not identify swaps inside exchanges. They are descriptive, not confirmatory.
- Robustness rows for the outcome: uncapped D and A; and D and A formed from USDC/B and USDT/B capped at one pooled pair of 1st/99th baseline percentiles across both legs (the 4 Sep pilot rule). A different decision case in any robustness row is reported and never overrides.

## 3. Confirmatory tests and decision rules

- **The pooled 35-day β is the only confirmatory estimate for H1.** H1a is confirmatory-secondary. H2 (prior-use intensity) is exploratory and reported as a pattern only.
- H1 is a fall in the capped USDC–USDT gap scaled by usual holdings, relative to matched non-exposed wallets. D mixes coin mix and wallet size, so H1 alone does not establish substitution or a USDT purchase.
- δ = 0.10 (H1, gap scale) and δₐ = 0.05 (H1a), fixed on economic grounds and never widened. As an accounting illustration on uncapped population coefficients, β_D = −0.10 with β_A = 0 corresponds to a +0.05 differential USDT balance and a −0.05 differential USDC balance, each scaled by usual holdings; with β_A anywhere in [−0.05, 0.05], the implied differential USDT change lies in [0.025, 0.075]. This is arithmetic on coefficients, not a guaranteed gain, a tested bound or a traced purchase.
- Dollar equivalents: at the median floor-passing exposed wallet (median of the 4,508 wallets' baseline mean holdings = $7,915.96; recomputed 2026-09-21 from `out/g1/cohort.csv`; the cohort mean is $133,461, so the median is the right reference), δ corresponds to a swap of about $396 and δₐ to about $396 leaving stablecoins.
- H1 decision rule. For a finite 95% interval [L, U] for β, classify in this order (the submitted draft's five cases; `R/decide.R`): (1) U < −δ: supports H1, subject to §2; (2) L ≤ −δ ≤ U: inconclusive; (3) L > −δ and U ≤ δ: a negligible differential response; (4) L > δ: a meaningful positive differential response; (5) otherwise: rules out a meaningful negative response and leaves the size of a positive one open. A missing or invalid interval is "not estimable", not one of the five cases. The wallet- and stratum-clustered intervals are each classified by this rule.
- H1a decision rule (95% interval), its own classifier, not the H1 one: wholly inside [−δₐ, δₐ] → a negligible differential change in combined on-chain holdings; wholly below −δₐ → a meaningful differential decline; wholly above δₐ → a meaningful differential increase; otherwise inconclusive.
- Reallocation wording. The results are described as "consistent with relative reallocation toward USDT on the observed addresses" only if all four hold: H1 is supported under §2 (both clusterings, veto included); the capped H1a interval lies wholly inside [−δₐ, δₐ]; the uncapped H1a interval does not lie wholly outside that band; and H1 is not flagged cap-sensitive. The sentence states that balances above the recorded caps are damped, and the uncapped legs are reported beside it. Otherwise the text reports only whether the gap fell, relative to non-exposed wallets, and reports the H1a result separately. No wording claims a traced swap, an absolute USDT increase among exposed wallets, a preference mechanism or a joint 95% region from separate intervals; none distinguishes exit from a move off the observed addresses (fresh addresses, swaps inside an exchange, other chains).
- Outflow breakdown: deferred by the author's scope decision of 27 October 2026. Its categories (ETH, decentralised exchanges, DAI, new addresses, overlaps between them, valuation) have no frozen definitions, and defining them after the post-event data is opened would be a post-hoc choice. Any later outflow analysis is exploratory, labelled as such, and never feeds the H1 or H1a decision.
- H2 intensity: the count of identified deposits (§1 depositor-resolution method) into the 23 pools up to 7 August 2022, deduplicated by transaction hash and log index; wallets with no identified deposit (withdrawal-only or unresolved) count zero. Cut-points *(pipeline records, Amendment A3)*: q₁ = 0, q₂ = 1, so the groups are zero, exactly one, and two or more identified deposits. Cut at the one-third and two-thirds empirical quantiles (type 7) among the final matched exposed wallets: low ≤ q₁, middle q₁ < count ≤ q₂, high > q₂. Ties are not split and groups are not forced to equal size; empty groups are reported. Category-by-Post interactions with the primary weights; H2 never determines H1.
- Power rule. Power = Φ(0.05/SE − 1.96), the planning probability that the interval clears −δ when β = −0.15, where SE is the larger of the wallet- and stratum-clustered SEs of the §2 H1 outcome in the rebuilt §5 item 2 placebo, not rescaled for window length. It is a planning proxy, not validated main-design power. Target 0.80. If power is below 0.80, or the rebuilt placebo is unavailable, the frame is enlarged once by exactly 30 further non-overlapping random 400-block baseline windows (seed 20251016, starts drawn uniformly without replacement from admissible baseline starts, overlaps rejected, block ranges recorded), keeping the original eligibility rules and cut-points and rematching at ≤ 3:1; the enlarged sample becomes primary even if its estimated power falls. Any remaining shortfall and the MDE δ + 2.80·SE are reported. The outcome, caps, thresholds and enlargement count are never changed to reach power. Power value *(pipeline records)*: 0.854 (larger SE 0.0166, stratum-clustered, filtered June placebo; MDE 0.1465), so the frame is not enlarged.

## 5a. Reporting rule

Every pre-specified row is reported, including rows that could not be estimated, with the reason. No sensitivity or robustness row replaces the primary estimate; material disagreements are reported as such. Unavailable screens are never described as completed.
